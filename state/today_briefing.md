# Daily AI Recruitment Briefing — 30 July 2026

## AI in Recruitment — What's New

- The Hackett Group released its **2026 Talent Acquisition Vendor Assessments**, evaluating 12 providers across 28 criteria and finding that AI and agentic automation are measurably speeding hiring — but adoption remains fragmented, with capabilities concentrated in point tools rather than end-to-end workflows. If you're renewing or consolidating your TA stack, use it as an independent benchmark for where agentic sourcing and CRM features are actually mature versus marketing. ([AI Transforms Talent Acquisition Tech](https://www.thehackettgroup.com/hackett-finds-rapid-advancement-in-ai-and-automation-transforms-talent-acquisition-technology/)) (29 July)
- **Buildforce raised a $10M Series A** (led by Saepio Capital) to scale its tech-enabled electrician staffing platform nationally, citing BlackRock data that projects the electrical trade as the fastest-growing US employment category over the next decade at ~9.5% annual growth. It's a signal that skilled-trades staffing — not just knowledge work — is drawing recruiting-tech capital; worth watching if your workforce plan leans on hard-to-source deskless or trades talent. ([Buildforce Raises $10M Series A to Staff Electricians Nationally](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/buildforce-raises-10m-series-a-to-staff-electricians-nationally-302837071.html)) (28 July)

## Worth Reading

- New research from The Conference Board (35 enterprise-leader interviews plus a survey of ~1,300 workers) finds **most organizations are upskilling employees for today's AI tasks, not the roles AI will reshape next** — a short-term training posture that leaves future capability gaps unaddressed. For TA and workforce planners, the takeaway is to tie reskilling and hiring plans to where roles are heading, not just current tool proficiency. ([Most Organizations Are Preparing Workers for Today's AI, Not Tomorrow's Jobs](https://www.conference-board.org/press/ai-skilling)) (28 July)

**Bottom line:** Capital and vendor capability are flowing toward agentic recruiting and skilled-trades staffing, but both the Hackett and Conference Board findings point the same way — the advantage goes to TA leaders who buy and skill for where roles are heading, not for today's fragmented tooling.
